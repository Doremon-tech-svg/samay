# SAMAY Right-to-Service Source Inventory and Implementation Backlog

## Purpose and evidence boundary

This report synthesizes the 36 jurisdiction results supplied for SAMAY. It distinguishes facts verified in the cited official materials from gaps, uncertain coverage, and research/access failures. Service counts are reported only where the supplied verification result explicitly supports an apparent count; they are not normalized into a purported current statewide total. No service rows, deadlines, officer names, or legal effects are added beyond the supplied evidence. Every jurisdiction-specific factual statement below is cited to its supplied evidence URLs. The source inventory records the source type and its coverage, deadline semantics, authority availability, access limitations, and confidence.

“Not verified” means that the materials actually read did not establish the fact; it does not mean that no notification or authority exists. “Apparent” counts describe what a cited portal/document says or displays and do not imply current legal completeness unless the source result explicitly supports that conclusion.

## Executive synthesis

The evidence ranges from readable service schedules and directories (for example, Assam, Haryana, Goa, Maharashtra, Odisha, Uttar Pradesh, and department-specific schedules) to framework Acts or rules without a verified service schedule (for example, Bihar, Jharkhand, Rajasthan, and Punjab), and to cases where even operative provisions or service details could not be read. Sources also differ in legal status: a portal directory, department-specific notification, draft regulation, tentative list, Act, Gazette rule, and general citizen charter are not interchangeable. These distinctions are preserved in the inventory rather than collapsed into a single service dataset. [Assam list](https://art.assam.gov.in/portlet-innerpage/artps-list-of-notified-public-services); [Haryana directory](http://haryana-rtsc.gov.in/notify-serviceslist); [Goa directory](https://dpg.goa.gov.in/time-bound-services/search/); [Odisha portal](https://ortpsa.odisha.gov.in/); [UP list](https://upcmo.up.nic.in/janhit.htm); [Bihar Act](https://bipard.bihar.gov.in/static/media/ACT.eac43e0a1b6572a7c35d.pdf); [Punjab notifications](https://digi.punjab.gov.in/actNotification); [Andaman and Nicobar draft](https://andamannicobar.gov.in/admin-pannel/whatsnew/3445-Press%20Note%20Draft%20Regulation%20RTS.pdf); [Ladakh tentative list](https://ladakh.gov.in/document/notification-of-services-in-terms-of-public-service-guarantee-act-2011-in-union-territory-of-ladakh/).

Several sources establish general statutory process but delegate the actual services, officers, and service-specific limits to later notifications. Conversely, some portals display service fields or counts without establishing that the list is a current, legally consolidated register. Day-count conventions and clock starts are especially non-uniform: the evidence includes explicit working-day definitions, working-day labels, bare “days,” trigger-specific periods, and sources where semantics are unavailable. SAMAY should therefore store deadline semantics per source/notification/row, never infer a global convention. [Gujarat Act index and Gazette](https://artd.gujarat.gov.in/rcps-act); [Arunachal Pradesh Rules](https://www.law.arunachal.gov.in/api/content/download/1789716092937-07a5ea3f557400ff4a2ee9a89cf343bf.pdf); [Bihar Act](https://bipard.bihar.gov.in/static/media/ACT.eac43e0a1b6572a7c35d.pdf); [Tripura notification](https://tripura.gov.in/sites/default/files/Notification%20from%20Revenue%20dept.pdf).

## Implementation backlog for SAMAY

### Stage 0 — Evidence-preserving source registry (first)

Create one jurisdiction record per the 36 jurisdictions in this report, with source records rather than premature service rows. Required fields: jurisdiction identifier/name; canonical official URL and all evidence URLs; issuing body; source title; document type; publication/issue date when verified; apparent scope; source status (`operative`, `amendment`, `draft`, `tentative`, `portal-directory`, `framework-only`, `unknown`); retrieval timestamp; access outcome; extraction method; confidence; and reviewer notes. Preserve raw document/page snapshots and checksums where permitted. Record competing versions and never overwrite a prior source silently. For example, keep Odisha’s 445- and 451-labelled PDFs as distinct source versions and Ladakh’s tentative annexure separate from any later final notice. [Odisha 451 PDF](https://ortpsa.odisha.gov.in/assets_web/website/Notificationof451numberofPublicServicesunderORTPSA.pdf); [Odisha 445 PDF](https://ortpsa.odisha.gov.in/assets_web/website/Notification_of_445_number_of_Public_Services_under_ORTPSA_as_on_16.12.2025.pdf); [Ladakh annexure](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s395192c98732387165bf8e396c0f2dad2/uploads/2023/05/2023052634.pdf).

### Stage 1 — Catalog metadata and coverage labels

Before extracting services, catalog only verified metadata: what the source claims to cover, whether it is statutory or informational, apparent count if explicitly displayed, stated “as of” date, department/agency scope, presence of service/officer/appeal/deadline columns, and whether the document is consolidated. Label aggregate counts as source-reported and date-bound, not canonical totals. Mark department-specific lists distinctly from jurisdiction-wide sources; examples include Kerala Irrigation, Tripura Excise, Chandigarh Civil Engineering, and the Chhattisgarh Panchayat table. [Kerala](https://irrigation.kerala.gov.in/rts); [Tripura](https://tripura.gov.in/sites/default/files/Notification%20from%20Revenue%20dept.pdf); [Chandigarh](https://chdengineering.gov.in/right-to-service); [Chhattisgarh](https://prd.cg.gov.in/Lsgyojna.aspx).

### Stage 2 — Service-row extraction only from verified machine-readable or Gazette sources

Extract service rows only from sources whose content and row structure have been verified—prefer official Gazette/notification text, reliable machine-readable schedules, or a clearly rendered official table. Each row must carry source URL, exact notification/page/table reference, extraction timestamp, source status, verbatim service title, normalized title (if reviewed), scope, designated officer, competent authority, first/second/review/revisional authority as separate role types, and the exact deadline text. Do not extract rows from inaccessible, OCR-corrupted, incomplete, draft, or merely indexed documents as though verified. OCR can propose candidates but cannot activate data without a human check against the page image or authoritative text. For example, the extracted tables in Arunachal Pradesh, Tripura, and Maharashtra have alignment/formatting cautions; the Ladakh document is tentative; and the unreadable MP list must yield no rows. ### Stage 3 — Provenance, semantic normalization, and review before activation

Keep verbatim source values alongside normalized data. Model deadline amount/unit, calendar-versus-working-day convention, clock-start trigger, completeness condition, exclusions/pauses, staged/event-triggered timing, acknowledgement rule, appeal filing period, appeal disposal period, and deemed-service-approval status independently. Use explicit states such as `verified`, `not stated`, `not verified`, `ambiguous`, and `not applicable`; do not map unknowns to zero, calendar days, or false. Store the legal basis and exact source location for each normalized field. Require reviewer approval before activating any extracted row, including review of OCR alignment, source status, supersession, scope, and effective date. This is essential because the sources include explicit working-day definitions (Gujarat), holiday exclusions (Arunachal Pradesh), bare day values (Tripura), and deemed approval confined to a particular Daman & Diu industry schedule. ### Stage 4 — Jurisdiction-specific deadline and authority models
